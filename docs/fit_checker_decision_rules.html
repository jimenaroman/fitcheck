Step 1: one requirement gets a status

Runs on every required and preferred item. Read top to bottom. The first question that answers yes decides the outcome; otherwise, move to the next.

  1. R1

    Is it a soft skill?

    "Strong communication," "passion for technology," "team player."

    Not scored

    Shown in details. Never counted toward the score or the leaderboard.

  2. R2

    Is it an eligibility item?

    Graduation window, enrollment, GPA minimum, work authorization, location.

    To step 2

    Checked for eligibility, not skills.

  3. R3

    Does one requirement name more than one skill?

    Each named skill first gets its own status from R4 to R8. Then two separate things happen.

    For your score, the requirement counts once. With "or" (Java or C++), it's covered if you have at least one. With "and" (Python and Kubernetes), it's covered only if you have all of them.

    For the leaderboard, every named skill counts on its own, whether the posting used "and" or "or." Skills you have go to Strengths in demand. Skills you lack go to Most-requested gaps, even when the "or" was already covered by another skill.

    Split into skills

    Scored as one requirement. Tallied as separate skills.

  4. R4

    Did the model cite a bullet, and does the quote appear word for word in it?

    verify.py checks that the quote exists. Whether the bullet actually proves the requirement is the model's judgment, guided by rules M1 to M6 below.

    Have

    Evidence: the bullet ID and quote.

  5. R5

    Did the model cite something that isn't there?

    Quote not in the bullet, or an ID that doesn't exist.

    Gap

    Logged as a caught false match. Feeds P3 and your eval.

  6. R6

    Is the only evidence a course?

    When the posting asks for knowledge or understanding ("knowledge of statistics," "understanding of data structures"), a matching course counts as Have (M4). When it asks for hands-on experience ("experience building"), coursework alone is Weak.

    Have Weak

    Label: coursework.

  7. R7

    Is it only in your skills list?

    evidenced_by is empty, like SQL or TypeScript today. It's on your resume but nothing shows it, which is a different fix from a skill you've never listed.

    Weak

    Label: listed, not shown.

  8. R8

    None of the above

    Nothing on your resume supports it.

    Gap
  9. R9

    Does it ask for years of experience?

    "2+ years of Java." If the skill would be Have, it stays Have only when the cited quote itself states a duration that meets the requirement, like "2+ years building Java applications." Otherwise it drops to Weak. Code checks the quote for the duration. Years are never calculated from your resume dates.

    Have Weak

    Label: years not shown.

  10. R10

    Does it use a level word?

    "Familiarity," "proficiency," and "strong" are ignored. "Expert," "deep," and "advanced" are recorded. Level words never filter a posting out; only explicit degree requirements do (E3).

    Recorded, not shown

    Decide how to display it after 15 labeled postings.

R10 is a modifier (dashed): it never changes a status, and for now it's saved with each requirement but not displayed.

How the model judges evidence

Your calibration answers, turned into rules for the matching prompt. The principle behind all six: the more specific the posting, the more specific your evidence has to be. The model only judges what's written on your resume, never what you know.

IDRuleYour calibration caseStatus
M1A broad category is satisfied by any concrete example of real work in it."Web development" and the Streamlit appHave
M2Teaching a skill counts as having it."Strong foundation in Java" and tutoringHave

Step 2: eligibility items get checked

The model only reads the posting's facts, like a date window or a GPA number. Python compares them with your resume and profile.yaml.

  1. E1

    Graduation window

    Your expected date (May 2029) falls inside the window: eligible. Only your early date (Dec 2028) falls inside: unclear. Window ends before yours, like Dec 2026 – Jun 2027: Next year. Window starts after yours, like a program only for 2030 grads: filtered out. No window listed: eligible.

    Eligible Unclear Next year Filtered out
  2. E2

    Class-year wording

    Translated using the internship's season. For Summer 2027, "rising junior" and "current sophomore" both mean you.

    Eligible

    Otherwise ineligible or unclear, same as E1.

  3. E3

    Degree or enrollment

    "Pursuing a BS in CS or related field" or "or equivalent experience" is satisfied by edu-state. Postings only for MS or PhD students are an instant no.

    Eligible Filtered out
  4. E4

    GPA minimum

    Ignored. GPA minimums don't limit you the way degree requirements do, so they never filter, flag, or block a posting. The line still shows in the details.

    Ignored
  5. E5

    Work authorization

    Read from profile.yaml. "No visa sponsorship" passes because you're authorized.

    Eligible
  6. E6

    Location

    Off by default: location never affects eligibility. When you turn it on in profile.yaml: a posting is eligible if any of its locations is in one of your metros (SF, Chicago, NYC, Dallas–Plano) or it's remote. Otherwise it's filtered out, with the city as the reason. A location code can't recognize is unclear. No location listed: eligible.

    Ignored when off Eligible Filtered out Unclear
  7. E7

    Combine all checks into one status

    Checked in this order: any filtered-out check filters the posting out. Otherwise, any Next year check saves it to Next year. Otherwise, any unclear check makes it unclear. Otherwise, eligible.

    One overall status

Filtered postings aren't deleted. Checking the Filtered out list now and then catches a posting hidden by mistake, which a flag would never hide. Tapping a card opens the details: every required and preferred item with its status, its evidence, its flags, and each eligibility check.

What feeds the skills leaderboard

Each requirement carries normalized skill names, so "Proficiency in Python" and "Python 3.x" both count as python.

IDRule
L1A skill counts once per posting, no matter how many times the posting mentions it.
L2A duplicate posting (the same text) counts once.
L3Postings sent to review manually don't count.
L4Soft skills and eligibility items don't count.
L5Next year postings count, because they show what employers want from your field. Filtered-out postings don't count, because MS and PhD roles ask for research skills that would skew your list. You decide
L6Required and preferred are counted separately. A preferred gap gets highlighted when more than half of postings ask for it.
L7How often a skill is asked for never changes. Your status for it is recomputed from your current resume, so adding a project moves skills between boards.
L8Combined requirements are tallied skill by skill (R3). A skill you lack from an "or" you already covered still counts as a gap, marked "as an alternative," so you can tell it apart from a skill that blocked you.
L9Tapping "I've done this" on a gap moves that skill to Not on your resume yet. It still counts as a Gap in your score, because a reader can't see it either. Once a bullet proves it, the next recheck moves it to Strengths.
